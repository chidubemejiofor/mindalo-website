Checklist to take to the health worker

About 10 minutes · Fill it in, then print it

This checklist is only in English at the moment. The rest of the site is in eight languages.

This checklist helps you describe what you have noticed, in the order a health worker will want to hear it. Appointments are often short, and it is hard to remember everything while you are worried. Filling this in beforehand means nothing important is left out. Take it with you, or read from it.

The main points

  • This is not a test and it does not give a diagnosis.
  • Fill it in with someone who knows the person well.
  • Tick only the changes that are new, or that are getting worse.
  • Print it and take it to the appointment.

Get help the same day if confusion comes on suddenly

If someone becomes confused over hours or a few days, that is usually not dementia. It is more often an infection, a reaction to medicine, dehydration or a stroke. These need treating quickly. Go to a health worker straight away rather than filling in this checklist.

Nothing you write here is sent anywhere or saved. It stays on your phone or computer until you close the page, so print it or copy it onto paper before you leave.

9. Other health conditions and medicines

Some conditions change the picture, and some are treatable. Tell the health worker about high blood pressure, diabetes, stroke, HIV, epilepsy, poor hearing or sight, head injury, or heavy alcohol use. List every medicine the person takes, including ones bought without a prescription and herbal ones.

10. Anything else you have noticed

Write anything that worries you that is not on this list. One clear example is more useful than a general description, so give the day it happened and what the person did.

11. Questions to ask, and space for the answers

  1. What do you think is causing these changes?
  2. Which tests can be done here, and which need a bigger hospital?
  3. Could something treatable be causing this, such as an infection, a thyroid problem or a vitamin shortage?
  4. What can we do at home to help?
  5. Who do we contact if things get worse?
  6. When should we come back?

This checklist is for information only. It is not a test, it does not give a diagnosis, and it is not a substitute for seeing a health worker. Only a trained health worker can say what is causing these changes.
