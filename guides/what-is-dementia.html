Guide for carers

What is dementia?

About 4 minutes to read · Last checked September 2026

The main points

  • Dementia is caused by diseases that damage the brain.
  • It affects memory, thinking, talking and doing everyday tasks.
  • It usually gets worse over time.
  • It is not a normal part of getting old, and it is nobody’s fault.

What happens in dementia

Our brain controls how we remember, think, speak, move and feel. In dementia, disease slowly damages the brain. Over time, this makes daily life harder.

Dementia is different for every person. At first the changes can be small. Later, the person will need more help.

The main types of dementia

“Dementia” is a word for a group of symptoms. Different diseases can cause it. The most common are:

  • Alzheimer’s disease — the most common type. Memory problems are often the first sign.
  • Vascular dementia — caused by problems with blood flow to the brain, for example after strokes.
  • Dementia with Lewy bodies — can cause changes in movement, sleep, and seeing things that are not there.
  • Frontotemporal dementia — often changes personality, behaviour or language first. It can start at a younger age.

Some people have more than one type.

Who gets dementia?

Dementia is most common in people over 65. But younger people can get it too.

Many people with dementia also live with other health conditions, such as high blood pressure or diabetes. Looking after these conditions well is an important part of dementia care.

Can we lower the risk?

Nobody can promise to prevent dementia. But these things can lower the risk:

  • Keep your body active.
  • Do not smoke, and drink little or no alcohol.
  • Eat a healthy diet and keep a healthy weight.
  • Keep blood pressure, blood sugar and cholesterol under control.
  • Spend time with other people, and keep your mind busy.
  • Get help for hearing loss.

This guide gives general information. It does not replace advice from a doctor, nurse or health worker who knows the person.
