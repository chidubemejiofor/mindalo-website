Everyday care at home

About 6 minutes to read · Last checked September 2026

The main points

  • Speak slowly and calmly, and give the person time to answer.
  • A regular daily routine helps the person feel safe.
  • Make sure they eat well and drink enough water.
  • Changes in behaviour often have a cause, such as pain, hunger or needing the toilet.

Talking together

  • Face the person and use their name.
  • Speak slowly and calmly. Use short sentences.
  • Ask one question at a time. Give them time to answer.
  • Try not to argue or correct them. Their feelings are real, even when the facts are mixed up.
  • Use touch, smiles and gestures as well as words.

A regular routine

  • Try to do things at the same time each day: waking, meals, washing and sleep.
  • Let the person do as much as they can for themselves, even if it takes longer.
  • Keep up the things they enjoy — music, prayer, walks, time with family.
  • Keep days active and nights calm, to help with sleep.

Eating and drinking

  • Offer water and drinks often. People with dementia may forget to drink.
  • Serve small meals, and foods they can eat with their fingers if cutlery is hard.
  • Check their mouth, teeth or dentures if they stop eating. Pain can be the reason.
  • Sit and eat with them if you can.

Safety at home

  • Keep floors clear, and use a light at night so they can find the toilet.
  • Keep medicines, chemicals and sharp things locked away.
  • Take care with cooking fires, stoves and hot water.
  • Put the person’s name and a family phone number in their pocket, in case they get lost.
  • Let neighbours know, so they can help if the person is out alone.

When behaviour changes

A person with dementia may become upset, restless or angry. This is not their fault. Often something is wrong that they cannot put into words. Check if they are:

  • in pain, hungry, thirsty or tired
  • needing the toilet
  • too hot or too cold
  • scared by noise, crowds or a strange place

Once you find the cause, you can often ease the distress. Stay calm and speak gently. A calm voice helps the person feel safe, even if they do not follow the words. If that does not help, try a change of activity or a quieter place.

Get medical help

Sudden changes need medical help. Get help if the person suddenly becomes much more confused, very sleepy or unwell. These changes can be a sign of an infection or another illness. Quick treatment can help the person recover.

This guide gives general information about dementia. It is not medical advice for any one person. A doctor, nurse or health worker who knows the person can give advice that fits them. Please speak to them about any worries.
